BARBERSHOP CRM & AI
Product Vision & Requirements — Initial Source of Truth
Version 1.0 | 2026-09-06

1. PRODUCT VISION
The product is a SaaS system for managing a barbershop.
Its primary goal is to reduce the owner's/admin's time spent managing appointments
and customers, while giving customers a convenient self-service experience.

Core value proposition:
The product sells time. The barber should be able to open the system in the
morning, immediately understand the day's schedule, and spend much less time
answering WhatsApp messages, coordinating appointments, and manually managing
customer data.

AI is an assistant, not the source of truth. Business rules, availability,
permissions, and database state remain deterministic and controlled by the app.

The project began as a general appointment-management project for an AI Agents
course, then became a specialized barbershop system with Gemini, ChromaDB and RAG.

2. TARGET CUSTOMER
Primary customer: an independent barbershop owner/head barber who manages one or
more additional barbers/staff members. The system should also support adding
staff later, including different service types such as a women's barber/stylist.

This is NOT a marketplace for discovering random barbershops.

3. BUSINESS / TENANT MODEL
The barbershop is the business/tenant.

Barbershop
- Owner / Main Manager
- Staff / Secondary Barbers
- Customers
- Appointments
- Services
- Business settings
- Products (later)

Owner permissions:
- Manage the entire business
- View/manage all appointments
- Manage customers and staff
- Add/edit/remove/move appointments
- Configure services, prices, business policies and schedules
- View business summaries/analytics

Secondary staff permissions:
- Primarily manage their own calendar
- View their own working hours and days off
- Only access information they are authorized to access
- Must not automatically receive owner-level access

Critical architecture requirement:
Data belonging to one barbershop must be isolated from every other barbershop.

4. ONBOARDING
When an owner registers/purchases the system, onboarding should guide them
through configuring:
- Business information
- Staff
- Services
- Service duration
- Service prices
- Working days/hours
- Breaks
- Booking policy
- Cancellation policy
- Other business policies

Exact onboarding UX is still to be designed.

5. CUSTOMER TYPES
Registered Customer:
- Has an account
- Can log in
- Can manage appointments
- Has a profile and history

Guest / Temporary Customer / Lead:
- Can book without registering
- Booking information is stored
- Business can see them as a lead/customer candidate
- Later, the temporary record can be linked/merged with the registered account

Matching should use reliable information such as phone number and potentially
additional verification data. Avoid duplicate customer records.

Current useful tags/statuses:
- Unregistered / Lead
- Birthday this month

Guest conversion is part of the long-term CRM/marketing vision. Example:
a guest books without an account; later the business can send a WhatsApp offer
encouraging registration, potentially with 10% off the next haircut.

6. CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE
Customers should have a dedicated interface and be able to:
- Register/login
- Book appointments
- Choose a barber when applicable
- Choose one or more services
- View upcoming appointments
- View appointment history
- Reschedule/cancel according to policy
- Ask general business questions
- Ask about services, prices, hours and policies
- Use natural language through the customer AI assistant

The customer should not need to wait for the barber to answer WhatsApp for routine
questions or scheduling.

7. APPOINTMENT MANAGEMENT
Appointment management is core.

Customers:
- Book appointments
- Choose a barber when multiple barbers exist
- Potentially choose "any available barber" (future design)
- Choose multiple services
- Book for multiple people
- Book up to three months ahead

Owner/barber:
- Create, edit, move and delete/cancel appointments
- Create walk-in appointments
- Override certain scheduling situations as an authorized manager

Manager-created conflicts should generate a warning rather than necessarily
hard-blocking the action. Exact safeguards need design to prevent mistakes.

Availability must consider:
- Barber
- Service duration
- Working hours
- Breaks
- Days off
- Blocked time
- Existing appointments
- Booking window
- Business closure dates
- Other scheduling rules

The application, not the LLM, determines actual availability.

8. SERVICES
Each service should support at least:
- Name
- Price
- Duration

Example:
Haircut + Beard = 35 minutes / 100 NIS
Haircut = 25 minutes / configurable price

Owner can create/edit services.

Services are predefined for customers. Authorized staff can make special
additions, such as adding a hair wash to an appointment. The extra service
increases duration and must not create a conflict with the next appointment.
Only the barber/authorized staff can make such an exception.

9. WORKING HOURS / BREAKS / DAYS OFF
Each barber can have their own availability.

Support:
- Working days
- Working hours
- Breaks
- Days off
- Holidays/closure dates
- One-time blocked periods
- Different schedules by day

Customers must never be offered slots during unavailable periods.

Future integration with Google Calendar or a similar calendar is desired to
account for holidays/special events and possible conflicts. Exact integration
rules are not yet decided.

10. CANCELLATIONS
Business cancellation policy should be configurable.

Current intended example:
- Less than 4 hours before the appointment: customer contacts the barber.
- Barber cancels manually.
- Otherwise the customer can cancel according to policy.
- Barber receives a notification when a customer cancels.

11. WAITING LIST
A waiting list is desired.
When a slot opens:
- Consider eligible people on the waiting list
- Work through the first five in sequence
- Notify the barber about the opportunity

Exact customer notification/offer flow is still to be designed.

12. APPOINTMENT STATUS
The system needs enough status to manage the day and record attendance.
Important states include scheduled/upcoming, completed/attended and cancelled.
The barber should be able to mark whether the customer showed up.

No-show infrastructure may be prepared, but no-show intelligence is not an
initial priority. Customer lateness is not a core feature.

13. CUSTOMER CRM
Core customer data may include:
- Name
- Phone
- Registration status
- Appointment/visit history
- Spending/revenue information
- Next appointment
- Tags
- Birthday
- Optional notes

Potential customer profile:
David Cohen
Phone: 050-XXXXXXX
Visits: 18
Last visit: 02/09
Total spent: 1,440 NIS
Average visit: 80 NIS
Next appointment: 10/09 17:30

Customer notes are possible but their usefulness should be evaluated.
Loyalty programs are NOT required initially.

14. BARBER DASHBOARD
The dashboard should be smart, intuitive and simple.

Minimum goals:
- Immediately understand today's schedule
- See upcoming appointments
- Manage the calendar
- Create future appointments
- View appointment history
- View the previous month's history
- See useful business summaries

Potential metrics:
- Today's appointments
- Remaining appointments
- Expected revenue
- Completed appointments
- Cancellations
- Available slots
- Weekly/monthly summaries

The exact first-screen layout is not yet decided and should be researched.

15. REVENUE / ANALYTICS
Owner should receive business summaries:
- Expected revenue today/week/month
- Appointment counts
- Customer counts
- New customers
- Cancellations
- Other useful KPIs

Distinguish expected revenue from booked appointments and actual revenue from
completed transactions.

Real payment processing is NOT required initially.

16. PRICE CHANGES
Current desired behavior:
- If an appointment is less than one week away when the price changes, preserve
  the old price for that appointment.
- If the appointment is more than one week away, notify the customer via WhatsApp
  that the price changed.
- Owner can send a general price-update message to customers with an effective date.

17. DURATION CHANGES
If a service duration changes, the current desired behavior is to update affected
appointments. This must be implemented carefully because it can create conflicts
and therefore requires explicit testing.

18. CUSTOMER AI ASSISTANT
Natural-language customer requests should include examples such as:
- "Do you have anything available tonight?"
- "When is my next appointment?"
- "Cancel my appointment tomorrow."
- "I want a haircut with Daniel on Thursday."
- "How much does haircut and beard cost?"
- "Are you open on Friday?"

The assistant should help with booking, appointment checks, cancellation,
potential rescheduling, and general business questions.

It must not expose other customers' information.
If information is unknown, it should say so and direct the customer to the barber.
It must not invent prices, policies, availability or customer data.

19. CUSTOMER AI AUTHENTICATION / CONFIRMATION
Sensitive customer data/actions require reasonable identity verification.

Desired balance:
- Convenient enough for normal users
- Strong enough to prevent easy unauthorized access

For sensitive actions such as cancellation, use a two-step confirmation flow.
Security must be enforced by backend identity/authorization, not by prompts alone.

20. BARBER / OWNER AI ASSISTANT
The owner/barber gets a separate smart assistant.

Potential read-oriented questions:
- "What appointments do I have today?"
- "How many customers do I have this week?"
- "How much revenue is expected this week?"
- "How many new customers did I get this month?"
- "Find David."
- Other useful operational questions

Current decision:
AI should NOT directly perform destructive/modifying actions.
Future flow may be:
AI request -> explicit confirmation -> deterministic business logic -> database.

21. PROACTIVE NOTIFICATIONS
Fully autonomous proactive AI is not currently required.

Automated non-AI notifications are desired.
Example:
"Good morning [Barber Name]. Today you have [X] appointments: ..."
with the day's appointment list and times.

WhatsApp is the preferred channel. Email may be used where appropriate.

22. WHATSAPP
Future WhatsApp use cases:
- Appointment confirmations
- Cancellation notifications
- Price-change notifications
- Morning schedule summaries
- Guest-to-registered marketing
- Birthday messages
- Waiting-list notifications
- Other business communications

The actual integration is later. Notification delivery should ideally be
abstracted so WhatsApp is not hard-coded throughout business logic.

23. PAYMENTS / INVOICES
Long-term vision:
Appointment -> Service completed -> Payment -> Invoice -> Customer history -> Actual revenue

Initial version:
- Prepare useful underlying architecture
- Demonstrate the flow
- Do not implement real payment processing yet

24. PRODUCTS / INVENTORY

25. GOOGLE CALENDAR / EXTERNAL CALENDARS
Future integration with Google Calendar or similar.
Goals:

26. MARKETING / CUSTOMER RETENTION
Long-term opportunities:

27. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES
1. Database and deterministic Python/business logic are the source of truth.
2. LLM is a language/interface layer, not the authority over business rules.
3. Availability is calculated deterministically.
4. Permissions are enforced by the backend.
5. AI receives only authorized data.
6. Never rely on prompts alone for security.
7. Destructive actions require explicit confirmation.
8. Multi-tenancy is an architectural requirement.
9. Introduce features incrementally and test them.
10. Avoid unnecessary AI calls when deterministic code is enough.
11. Prepare abstractions for future integrations without implementing everything now.
12. Keep clear separation between CRM, appointment logic, auth, AI/NLU, RAG and notifications.

31. DEVELOPMENT AUDIT PLAN
Before adding major features, compare this document against the existing code.

Classify every major area:
KEEP = correct and aligned
REFACTOR = useful but architecturally needs improvement
REPLACE = conflicts with intended architecture/product
REMOVE = legacy/duplicate/unnecessary
MISSING = required but not implemented

Audit order:
1. Frontend
2. FastAPI routes
3. Authentication/authorization
4. Appointment/business logic
5. CRUD/data access
6. Database/schema
7. Customer CRM
8. Multi-tenancy
9. AI/NLU
10. State machine
11. RAG/ChromaDB
12. Notifications
13. Tests
14. Security
15. Deployment readiness

Do not add major new features until the current implementation and source of
truth are understood.

32. WORKING METHOD
For every major change:
1. Understand existing implementation.
2. Explain the concepts simply.
3. Compare implementation to product requirements.
4. Decide KEEP / REFACTOR / REPLACE / REMOVE / BUILD.
5. Implement the smallest appropriate change.
6. Run tests.
7. Verify regressions.
8. Document important architectural decisions.
9. Move forward.

The project is both a real product intended for commercial use and a learning
project in which the developer should understand what is being built.
